# Mortgage Product Rules & Structures (Canada)

## 1. Fixed vs Variable Rate Basics

**Fixed-rate mortgages:**
- Interest rate is locked for the term (e.g., 1–5 years).
- Payments stay predictable during that term.
- Helpful for borrowers who value stability and budget certainty.
- Typically come with **interest rate differential (IRD)** or **3-month interest** penalties for breaking early.

**Variable-rate mortgages:**
- Rate is linked to the lender’s **prime rate** (e.g., prime ± spread).
- Payments or amortization may adjust as prime changes (depends on product).
- Can be cheaper if rates fall, but payments can rise if rates increase.
- Penalties often based on 3-month interest, but details vary by lender.

## 2. Common Terms

Typical term lengths:
- 1-year, 2-year, 3-year, 4-year, 5-year (most common), and sometimes longer.
- Shorter terms can work for:
  - Borrowers expecting rate changes or life changes soon.
- Longer terms are chosen for:
  - Maximum stability and predictability.

## 3. Amortization vs Term

- **Amortization** is the total time to pay off the mortgage (e.g., 25 or 30 years).
- **Term** is the length of time the current rate and conditions apply (e.g., 5-year term within a 25-year amortization).
- At the end of each term, borrower:
  - Renews with same lender, or
  - Switches/refinances to another lender.

## 4. Open vs Closed Mortgages

**Closed mortgage:**
- Limited prepayment flexibility (only certain % per year).
- Typically offers **lower interest rate** than fully open.
- Early payout triggers a penalty.

**Open mortgage:**
- Can be paid off in full at any time without penalty.
- Rate is usually higher.
- Often used short term while waiting for a sale, bonus, or other event.

## 5. Prepayment Privileges

Many fixed and variable products offer:
- Annual lump sum payments (e.g., 10–20% of original principal per year) without penalty.
- Ability to increase regular payments (e.g., by up to 10–20%).
- Some allow double-up payments or accelerated bi-weekly payment schedules.

Using prepayments:
- Can significantly reduce interest cost over time.
- Effectively shortens the amortization.

## 6. High-Ratio vs Conventional Product Differences

**High-ratio products** (insured):
- Must comply with insurer rules (maximum amortization, property type, etc.).
- May have slightly different pricing than conventional.
- Insurance premium is added to mortgage principal.

**Conventional products** (uninsured):
- More flexibility on amortization and property types in some cases.
- Rates can be similar or sometimes slightly higher/lower, depending on lender strategy.
- No insurance premium, but stronger down payment required.

## 7. Fixed vs Variable Strategy Considerations

When advising a client, consider:
- **Risk tolerance**: can they handle payment increases?
- **Time horizon**: how long they plan to keep the property or mortgage.
- **Income stability**: salaried vs variable income.
- **Economic outlook**: high-rate vs low-rate environment and where rates may trend.

## 8. Penalties for Breaking the Mortgage

For many fixed-rate mortgages:
- Penalty is the **greater of**:
  - 3 months’ interest, OR
  - Interest Rate Differential (IRD), based on:
    - Remaining term
    - Original rate vs current posted/discounted rates.
- For many variable-rate mortgages:
  - Penalty is often **3 months’ interest**.

Penalties can be significant and should always be considered when:
- Planning to move or refinance early.
- Comparing lenders or products.

## 9. Porting & Assumption

**Porting**:
- Some mortgages allow transferring the existing rate/terms to a new property, subject to approval.
- Can help avoid or reduce penalties.

**Assumption**:
- Certain mortgages may be assumable by a qualified buyer, but this is lender and product specific.

## 10. Special Product Types (High Level)

- **Cashback mortgages**: lender gives cash at closing in exchange for slightly higher rate.
- **Hybrid / combo mortgages**: part fixed, part variable.
- **Lines of credit secured on home (HELOC)**:
  - Often combined with a term mortgage in a “readvanceable” structure.
- These products have additional rules and risk considerations and are generally used with careful advice.
