# Mortgage FAQ & Common Questions (Canada)

## 1. “How much can I qualify for?”

Lenders estimate a **maximum mortgage amount** based on:
- GDS/TDS ratios,
- Stress-tested payment (qualifying rate),
- Credit, income stability, and debts.
No one number applies to everyone; it depends on the full application.

## 2. “What is the stress test and why does it matter?”

- The stress test requires borrowers to qualify at a higher rate than their actual contract rate.
- This ensures they can handle future rate increases.
- It directly affects the **maximum amount** they can borrow.

## 3. “What down payment do I need?”

- Under **$500,000**: minimum 5%.
- Between **$500,000 and $999,999**: 5% of first $500,000 + 10% of the rest.
- **$1,000,000 or more**: typically 20% minimum.
- Investment and rental properties usually require higher down payments.

## 4. “What documents will I need?”

Common documents:
- Government-issued **ID**.
- **Pay stubs** (recent 2–3 months).
- **Employment letter** confirming position, income, and status.
- **Bank statements** (2–3 months) showing income deposits and down payment source.
- **Tax documents** (e.g., T4s, Notices of Assessment), especially for self-employed.

## 5. “How long does the mortgage approval process take?”

Typical timeline:
- Initial pre-approval: from same day to a few days.
- Full approval with documents and appraisal: often 5–10 business days, depending on:
  - Complexity of the file,
  - How quickly documents are provided,
  - Appraisal or insurer turn times.

## 6. “What’s the difference between pre-approval and final approval?”

- **Pre-approval**:
  - Based on preliminary information and credit check.
  - Gives an estimated maximum amount and often a rate hold.
- **Final approval**:
  - Based on full documents, property details, and insurer sign-off (if applicable).
  - Only at this stage is financing truly confirmed.

## 7. “Should I choose fixed or variable?”

Depends on:
- Comfort with payment changes (risk tolerance).
- Budget flexibility.
- View on future interest rates.
- Time horizon for staying in the property or mortgage.
Many borrowers choose fixed for stability; others accept variability in exchange for potential savings.

## 8. “Can I pay off my mortgage early?”

- Most closed mortgages allow **prepayments**, such as:
  - Annual lump sum (e.g., up to 10–20% of original principal).
  - Increasing regular payment amount.
- Paying off or breaking the mortgage entirely can trigger penalties; always check the product’s prepayment rules.

## 9. “What happens if I break my mortgage before the term ends?”

- You will usually pay a **penalty**:
  - For many fixed mortgages: greater of 3 months’ interest or IRD.
  - For many variable mortgages: often 3 months’ interest.
- Some products are more flexible; some are very strict.

## 10. “Will changing jobs affect my approval?”

- Lenders like **stable employment**.
- A recent job change is not automatically a problem, but:
  - Frequent job changes, probationary status, or variable income can lead to more questions.
  - Self-employed income usually requires more documentation and a longer history.

## 11. “How do my other debts affect my mortgage amount?”

- All monthly debt payments count toward **TDS**.
- Higher car loans, credit card balances, and lines of credit reduce how much mortgage you can qualify for.
- Paying down debts before applying can increase borrowing capacity.

## 12. “Do I need perfect credit to get a mortgage?”

- Not necessarily, but:
  - Better credit generally means more options and better rates.
  - Late payments, collections, or prior insolvency can limit choices.
- Some “alternative” or “B-lenders” exist for weaker credit, but with higher rates and stricter terms.

## 13. “What if my documents don’t match what I told you verbally?”

- Underwriters rely on **documents**, not just stated information.
- If documents show lower income or higher debts than expected, the approval amount may be reduced.
- Consistency between application, documents, and credit report is critical.

## 14. “Can I include rental income to qualify?”

- Many lenders allow a portion of **rental income** to offset expenses or count toward income.
- Policies vary (e.g., percentage allowed, need for leases, proof of rent).
- For multi-unit properties or investment properties, rules may be stricter.

## 15. “What should I do before I start house shopping?”

Usually recommended:
- Get a **pre-approval**.
- Gather key documents (ID, income, down payment proof).
- Check your **credit report** for errors.
- Clarify your budget and comfort zone, not just the maximum the bank will allow.
