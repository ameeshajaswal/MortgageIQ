# Eligibility Policies & Underwriting Basics (Canada)

## 1. Key Ratios: GDS and TDS

- **Gross Debt Service (GDS)** measures housing costs vs. gross income.
- **Total Debt Service (TDS)** measures housing + all other debt vs. gross income.
- Common lender guideline:  
  - **GDS ≤ ~32%** of gross monthly income  
  - **TDS ≤ ~44%** of gross monthly income  
- Some lenders may allow slightly higher ratios for strong files (excellent credit, stable income, large down payment).

## 2. What Goes Into GDS

Typical items included in GDS calculation:
- Mortgage **principal + interest** payment
- **Property taxes**
- **Heating** costs (or standard estimate)
- Condo **fees** (usually 50% of monthly condo fees)

GDS formula (simplified):
- GDS = (P+I + taxes + heat + 50% condo fees) / gross monthly income.

## 3. What Goes Into TDS

TDS includes:
- All items in GDS **plus**
- Monthly **car payments / leases**
- **Credit cards** (often a percentage of limit if no set payment)
- **Lines of credit**, student loans, personal loans, etc.

TDS formula (simplified):
- TDS = (GDS costs + all other monthly debt payments) / gross monthly income.

## 4. Stress Test Rules (Mortgage Qualifying Rate)

- Borrowers are qualified at the **higher** of:
  - The **benchmark qualifying rate** (e.g., 5.25%), **or**
  - The **contract rate + 2%**.
- This is to ensure the borrower can still afford payments if rates increase.
- The qualifying rate can change over time; always treat it as a **policy variable**, not a fixed forever number.

## 5. Minimum Down Payment (Owner-Occupied)

- For **purchase price up to $500,000**: minimum down payment is **5%**.
- For **portion between $500,000 and $999,999**: down payment is:
  - 5% of first $500,000, **plus**
  - 10% of the portion above $500,000.
- For **$1,000,000+**: typically **20% minimum** and not insured by standard default insurers.

## 6. High-Ratio vs Conventional

- **High-ratio** mortgage:
  - Down payment **< 20%** of purchase price.
  - Requires mortgage default insurance (e.g., CMHC, Sagen, Canada Guaranty).
- **Conventional** mortgage:
  - Down payment **≥ 20%**.
  - No default insurance premium, but lender still underwrites risk.

## 7. Mortgage Default Insurance Basics

- Premium is usually added to the mortgage amount.
- Premium rate depends on:
  - Loan-to-value ratio (LTV)
  - Occupancy (owner-occupied vs rental)
  - Purpose (purchase vs refinance, new build vs resale).
- Insurers have their own rules on:
  - Maximum amortization
  - Property type
  - Credit score and income stability.

## 8. Employment & Income Requirements

Common underwriting expectations:
- **Full-time salaried**:
  - Typically 2–3 recent pay stubs + employment letter.
  - Sometimes T4 or Notice of Assessment for additional support.
- **Hourly or variable**:
  - Lender may use a 2-year average of income (including overtime/bonuses).
- **Self-employed**:
  - Often 2 years of full tax returns and/or NOAs.
  - Add-backs may include depreciation, some business expenses, etc.
- **Probationary periods** may be scrutinized more closely.

## 9. Credit & History

- Lenders review:
  - **Credit score** and **payment history**.
  - Recent delinquencies, collections, bankruptcies, consumer proposals.
  - Number of trade lines and their limits.
- Strong credit can sometimes compensate for slightly higher ratios; weak credit can lead to stricter limits.

## 10. Property & Location

- Underwriting also considers:
  - Property type (detached, semi, condo, rural, multi-unit).
  - Marketability and location risk.
  - Appraised value vs purchase price.
- Some properties or locations may be on **restricted** or **declined** lists for certain lenders or insurers.

## 11. Amortization Rules (Insured vs Uninsured)

- Insured mortgages usually have a **maximum amortization** (e.g., 25 years).
- Conventional / uninsured may allow a longer amortization (e.g., 30 years).
- Longer amortization lowers monthly payment but increases total interest.

## 12. General Principle

- Underwriting is a **balance** of:
  - Income + stability
  - Debts + ratios
  - Credit
  - Down payment & assets
  - Property risk
- No single factor decides everything; lenders view the **overall** risk profile.
